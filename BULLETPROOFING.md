## 1. Two changes I made to the brief, and why

### The brief said "banned five times in India." The slide says three.

The documented record is **three** bans: **1948** (after Gandhi's assassination, lifted about four months later after the RSS undertook to remain non-political), **1975** (during the Emergency, lifted 1977), and **1992** (after the Babri Masjid demolition). I could not source a fifth or even a fourth ban from any reliable outlet. Publishing "five" would have handed a critic a free, cheap correction on the one slide built entirely from institutional facts. The slide says three, with the years named, which is both verifiable and harder to argue with.

If you have a source for five, send it and I will change it back. Some counts add provincial or organization-specific restrictions, but those are not national bans and should not be described as such.

### The brief said "thousands of individual letters sent." The slide does not say this.

I could not verify a number. The letter-writing campaign existed and ran on Action Network, but the platform's counter was not reachable from this environment, and no news report I found stated a figure. Per your own instruction not to use unverified claims, I dropped it and used the verified detail instead: more than 40 organizations wrote **to the Minister of Public Safety**. Naming the recipient is more concrete than an unsourced volume figure, and it puts a specific office on the record.

If you can screenshot the campaign counter, that number can go back on slide 04.

### One more thing I changed: the slide 05 question

Your narrative arc for slide 05 ended with "an ideology built on the subordination of non-Hindu minorities." That is an editorial characterization, and your own guardrail says to frame the RSS using institutional language only. The two instructions conflict, so I followed the guardrail. The slide now asks what would have to be true for 21 charities to pass the public benefit test "while a US federal commission recommends sanctioning the movement the complaint says they fund." Same contradiction, same force, but every element is attributable, and "the complaint says" keeps the allegation attributed rather than asserted.

## 3. Legal footing

**The distinction that protects you.** The CRA complaint is an **allegation made by a complainant**. It is not a finding, a ruling, a charge, or a revocation. No named organization has been found to have done anything unlawful. Slide 03 states this in red on the same slide as the names, which is the single most important defensive element in the deck.

**Do not, in captions, comments, Stories, or replies, say any of the following:**

- that any named organization "funds the RSS" as established fact
- that any named organization is "under investigation" (the CRA has not confirmed it opened one)
- that any organization's charitable status has been revoked or suspended
- that the CIA "designates" anything in the present tense
- that USCIRF "sanctioned" anyone. It recommended sanctions. It has no power to impose them.

**Do say:**

- "a complaint asks the CRA to examine"
- "the filing identifies" / "the complaint alleges"
- "USCIRF recommended"
- "in 2018 the Factbook listed"

**Reporting on a regulatory filing is ordinarily protected**, and accurate attributed reporting on a public complaint is standard journalism. The protection depends on accuracy and on attribution. The deck maintains both. The risk rises the moment the framing shifts from "a complaint alleges" to "these charities fund the RSS." Keep the attribution in every downstream mention.

**Due process.** The deck names four organizations, all of which are entitled to respond. If any of them issues a denial or statement, the fair move is to note it in a Story or in Part III. That costs you nothing and materially strengthens the series against a bad-faith attack.

**One rendering note for future builds.** `wkhtmltoimage` is not installed here, so I rendered with Chromium. Chromium's `--headless` and `--headless=new` modes both produce a **1263px** viewport when asked for 1350, which silently pushed the footer and pips 87px up the page and cut the accent bar short. The `headless_shell` binary gives a true 1350px viewport. The render command that works:

```bash
/opt/pw-browsers/chromium_headless_shell-1194/chrome-linux/headless_shell \
  --disable-gpu --no-sandbox --hide-scrollbars --force-device-scale-factor=1 \
  --window-size=1080,1350 --screenshot=out.png file:///abs/path/slide.html
```

Or just run `python3 build_carousel.py && ./render.sh`.

Fonts are embedded in each HTML file as base64 woff2, so the files render identically anywhere, with no system font installation required.
