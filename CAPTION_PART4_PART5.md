## On the India block — what I changed, and what I can't promise

Part II was blocked. The most likely triggers in that deck were the combination of the organization's initials, the founder's name, the Golwalkar quotation, and the Nazi Germany comparison appearing together on one slide. That combination is the highest-value target for a takedown request under India's IT Rules.

**What I did in Part V:**

- **The Golwalkar quote and the Nazi Germany line are not in this deck at all.** They live in Part II. Repeating them here would re-trigger the same profile for no new argumentative gain.
- **Mohan Bhagwat is not named.** Part V is about structures, not the visit.
- **"Hindutva" does not appear.** Neither does the organization's expanded name.
- **Organization names appear once each, where the sourcing rail requires them**, then the deck refers to structures rather than repeating proper nouns.
- **Both captions are entirely free of proper nouns.** Captions are the most reliably machine-scanned surface.

**What I can't promise.** Image OCR is real and increasingly used for exactly this. Slide text is not a safe harbour. And there is a genuine tradeoff you should decide consciously: the organization names are what make the deck defensible against a defamation complaint. Strip them out entirely and the deck gets harder to block but easier to sue. I have kept them, once each, because I think legal defensibility is worth more than geographic reach here.

**If Part V gets blocked anyway**, the highest-leverage remaining lever is slide 02. Removing the organization name and the 1991 registration date, leaving only "21 registered charities" and the figures, would cut the keyword surface substantially at real cost to the sourcing. Your call, not mine.
