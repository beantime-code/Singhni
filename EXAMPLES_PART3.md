# How the Machine Works: A Hypothetical, and the Real Cases Behind It

This supplements Part III. It has three sections, and they are not interchangeable:

1. A **hypothetical** walkthrough, with fictional names, showing mechanically how the structure works.
2. **Real, decided CRA cases** that match the exact violation category alleged against the 21 charities: direction and control, conduit behaviour, non-qualified donees. None involve the organizations in this series. None involve terrorism.
3. One real case I looked at and am **deliberately not using**, and why.

Keep these separated if you publish any of this. Blending them is the one mistake that would undo the careful attribution the rest of the series relies on.

---

## 1. The hypothetical (not a real case, not the RSS-linked charities)

Fictional names throughout: **Unity Relief Foundation**, **Cultural Bridge Society**, **Regional Development Trust**. None of these exist. None are the four organizations named in the actual complaint.

**Placement.** Unity Relief Foundation is a registered Canadian charity. Over a year, a few thousand donors give a total of $2 million. Each gets a tax receipt. Combined federal and provincial charitable tax credits run roughly 30 to 50 percent depending on the province and the size of the gift, so for every dollar a donor gives, other Canadian taxpayers are covering a meaningful share of it through the treasury. That's not a flaw in the system. It's the deal: the public subsidizes giving because the activity is supposed to be charitable.

**Layering.** Unity Relief Foundation signs a grant agreement with Cultural Bridge Society, a nonprofit that is not itself a registered Canadian charity, a "non-qualified donee." Since the June 2022 rule change, Unity doesn't need to direct and control what Cultural Bridge Society does with the money, the way it would have before. It needs a written agreement and reasonable assurance the funds go toward a charitable-type purpose, reported on its T3010 and a form called the T1441. $1.8 million moves this way over the year. On paper, every step is compliant.

**Integration.** Cultural Bridge Society sends $1.5 million to Regional Development Trust, an organization in another country. Because Cultural Bridge Society was never itself a Canadian charity, the CRA's oversight effectively ends at the previous step. What Regional Development Trust does with the money, schools, wells, political organizing, something else entirely, is now outside Canadian regulatory visibility.

**End state:** one donor's $500 gift, roughly 40 percent of it paid by other Canadians through tax credits, passed through two intermediaries and left the country to an organization Canada has no jurisdiction over and, likely, no reason to ever look at again.

This is not illegal. It's the same pipeline that legitimately moves money to a hospital in a disaster zone. The structure is neutral. What it carries is the only question that matters, and the structure itself gives almost nobody a reason to check.

## 2. Real, decided cases (not hypothetical, not this series' organizations)

These four cases were investigated and decided. Each turned on exactly the failure pattern in the complaint against the 21 RSS-linked charities: failing to maintain direction and control, acting as a conduit, failing to devote resources to the charity's own activities. None involve any organization named in this series. None involve a terrorism finding.

| Charity | What CRA found | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Jewish War Veterans of Canada National Command (2004) | Lack of direction and control over funds provided to non-qualified donees | Registration revoked |
| Canadian Foundation for Masorti Judaism | CRA found the charity "purposefully acted as a conduit for non-qualified donees in Israel," transferring funds to organizations running their own separate activities without adequate charity oversight | Registration revoked |
| Ne'eman Foundation Canada | Failure to devote resources to the charity's own activities; lack of direction and control | Registration revoked |
| Universal Aide Society | Charity had not shown it devoted all its resources to its own charitable activities | Registration revoked, 2009 |

These are useful precisely because they're unremarkable. No headlines, no terrorism designation, no criminal charges. Just an audit, a finding, a revocation. That is what "the machine gets checked and doesn't hold up" actually looks like in the overwhelming majority of real cases. It is not a movie ending. It is a letter from the CRA and a charity that no longer exists.

## 3. The case I'm deliberately not using, and why

You will run into this one if you research further, so it's better you hear the reasoning from me first: **IRFAN-Canada**.

CRA revoked its charitable status in April 2011, citing over $14.6 million sent to operating partners run by Hamas officials. In April 2014, the federal government went further and listed IRFAN-Canada as a terrorist entity under the Criminal Code, meaning it became a criminal offence to deal in its property.

I'm not using this as "the" comparison case for this series, and I'd advise against it. IRFAN-Canada is a finding: investigated, decided, upheld through litigation, and confirmed at the level of criminal law. The complaint against the 21 charities in this series has produced none of that. It is a complaint the CRA has not ruled on. Reaching for IRFAN-Canada as an illustrative parallel would visually and rhetorically equate an open, unproven complaint with a completed terrorism-financing case, which is a categorically different and far larger claim than anything in your source material, and it is exactly the leap Part III's own disclaimer exists to block.

I'm telling you it exists, and why it doesn't belong in the deck or the blog post, rather than leaving you to find it yourself and wonder why I left it out.
