# The Same Year, The Same Country: A Budget Comparison

Two sets of numbers, both real, both from 2026. One is what Ottawa cut from the institutions that tell Canadians what's actually happening in their own country. The other is what a CRA complaint alleges moved through 21 charities. Neither is in dispute as a number. What they add up to, side by side, is worth sitting with.

**Standard caveat, stated once and meant throughout:** the CRA complaint is a complaint, not a finding. The CRA has not ruled. Every organization it names is entitled to due process. Nothing below asserts otherwise. This is a comparison of dollar figures, not a claim that one caused the other.

---

## What got cut this year

| Institution | Cut, fiscal 2026-27 | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| CBC/Radio-Canada | **$192 million** | Canada's public broadcaster: national and local news, in both official languages |
| Library and Archives Canada | **$11 million** this year (rising to $21.1M/year within three years; ~$50M over three years total) | The government's own record-keeper. $13.6 million of the savings comes specifically from its access-to-information and privacy unit, the office that processes Canadians' requests to see their own government's records |
| Statistics Canada | **~900 jobs** cut | Produces the data Canadians and businesses use to know what's actually happening in the country |

Fair context on Library and Archives Canada: alongside these cuts, the government also announced a separate, permanent $20 million annual investment aimed at systemic access-to-information backlogs across government. The picture there is mixed, cuts to the institution's own operations and staff, investment in the broader access-to-information system. Presented as reported, not cherry-picked.

## What the complaint alleges moved

| Figure | Amount |
|---|---|
| Moving between RSS-affiliated organizations inside Canada | **$46 million** |
| Sent from those organizations to RSS-linked groups abroad | **$21.5 million** |

These are not added together in this comparison. As noted elsewhere in this series, the press release doesn't make clear whether the international figure is a subset of the domestic one, so summing them risks double-counting. Each is compared on its own.

---

## The comparison, in plain terms

**This year's CBC cut is more than four times the entire domestic figure in the complaint.** $192 million cut from the public broadcaster. $46 million allegedly moving through 21 charities. Restoring less than a quarter of what Ottawa just cut from CBC would cover the whole domestic total the complaint describes.

**The same CBC cut is almost nine times the amount the complaint says left the country.** $192 million versus $21.5 million.

**What the complaint says left Canada is nearly double the entire Library and Archives Canada cut for the year.** $21.5 million versus $11 million. The institution responsible for the government's own records was ordered to cut less, this year, than the amount one complaint alleges moved out of the country through a handful of charities.

**And the closest match of all:** the federal government allocated $20.8 million to the Local Journalism Initiative for 2026-27, the program that funds journalist positions in underserved communities across Canada. The complaint's international figure, $21.5 million, is within a few percentage points of funding that entire national program for a year.

---

## What it could have bought

Not hypothetically. These are the actual, current, government-set prices for these actual, current programs.

- **$46 million** is more than double what Ottawa cut from Library and Archives Canada's entire budget this year, with enough left over to cover the CBC's cut roughly a quarter of the way.
- **$21.5 million** is close to a full year of the Local Journalism Initiative, funding reporters in the communities that have the fewest left.
- **$192 million**, the CBC cut alone, this year, is not comparable to either alleged figure. It's larger than both of them combined, several times over.

The question isn't whether these numbers are connected. They aren't, and this piece doesn't claim they are. The question is what it means that a government citing fiscal restraint to cut public broadcasting, public records, and public data can, in the same year, leave a $46 million complaint sitting uninvestigated.
